Small tools I make for the people around me. I plan each one, decide what it does and what it leaves out, and build it with AI.

  • Guide

    Fewer spam calls

    A step-by-step guide to fewer robocalls and spam calls, written so a grandparent could follow it.

    How I built it

    What I wanted: Free steps anyone could follow to get fewer spam calls, with an honest promise: fewer, not none.

    The problem: My first version was one long checklist, and it was too much for seniors.

    What I decided: Ask three questions first (your phone, your phone company, and whether you live in California), then show only the steps that fit, one per screen. Every fact comes from an official page, and it never asks for your phone number.

    The trade-off: Phone menus change often, so every step has to be checked again each time the guide changes.

  • Website

    This website

    Where my writing lives, in the same quiet look as my newsletter.

    How I built it

    What I wanted: My newest Substack posts on the Writing page, without updating it by hand.

    The problem: Substack’s security blocks GitHub’s computers, which publish this site, and the first three approaches failed.

    What I decided: Go through a reading service called Jina Reader. Each time the site is published, and once a day, GitHub reads my post list through it and adds the four newest. If that fails, the page shows a link to my Substack instead, so it never looks broken.

    The trade-off: A new post can take up to a day to show up.

  • Web app · In progress

    Quiet room

    A quiet place to read, study, or work alongside friends. No notifications, no games, no distractions.

    Step 1 of 11 is done. Made for a group from my church and shared with them by private link, so there’s no link here.

    How I’m building it

    What I wanted: Company while you read or study, without pings, games, or anything that punishes you for leaving.

    What I decided: You set your own status, like Focusing or On a break, and the app never checks what you’re doing. The first version leaves out chat, rankings, sound and real accounts.

    The trade-off: It runs on trust, which fits a room of friends.

The plans and code for all of these are public on GitHub.
