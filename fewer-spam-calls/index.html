Fewer spam calls

Five free steps that cut down robocalls and spam calls. Nothing stops them all, but these help.

Last checked: October 24, 2026

  1. Join the Do Not Call list

    • It’s free, and you only do it once. It never expires.
    • Online: go to DoNotCall.gov and register your phone number.
    • Or call 1-888-382-1222 from the phone you want to add. (TTY: 1-866-290-4236)
    • If you sign up online, you’ll get an email. Open it and click the link within 72 hours.
    • Sales calls can take up to 31 days to stop.

    Good to know: this stops honest companies. Scammers ignore it, so keep going with the next steps.

    Watch out: an email saying your registration is “expiring” is a scam.

  2. Turn on your phone company’s free spam blocking

    T-Mobile

    • Open your Phone app and dial #662#, then press call.
    • That turns on Scam Block. To turn it off, dial #632#.
    • Once in a while, it may block a call you wanted.

    Verizon

    • Verizon’s spam blocking is called Call Filter. The basic version is free.
    • Open the Verizon Protect app. Tap Services, then Call Filter, then Manage.
    • Turn on the spam filter.
    • No app? In the My Verizon app, go to Settings & preferences, then Call Filter.

    AT&T

    • Download the free AT&T ActiveArmor app.
    • It blocks fraud calls on its own.
    • In the app, choose to block spam calls or send them to voicemail.

    Another phone company

    • Call your phone company and ask: “Do you have free spam call blocking? Can you turn it on for me?”

  3. Turn on your phone’s own call screening

    iPhone

    • First, save your doctor, pharmacy and family as contacts, so their calls still ring.
    • Open Settings. Tap Apps, then Phone.
    • Find the setting for unknown callers and choose one:
      • “Ask Reason for Calling”: your iPhone asks callers you don’t know why they’re calling before it rings.
      • “Silence”: calls from numbers you don’t know go quietly to voicemail.

    Android, with Google’s Phone app (like a Pixel)

    • Open the Phone app. Tap More (the three dots), then Settings.
    • Tap Caller ID & spam. On some phones it’s called Spam and Call Screen.
    • Turn on spam filtering, or Call Screen if you see it.

    Samsung Galaxy

    • Open the Phone app. Tap the three dots, then Settings.
    • Tap Caller ID and spam protection, and turn it on.
    • Turn on Block spam and scam calls.

  4. Live in California? Use DROP

    • DROP is a free tool from the State of California.
    • One request asks over 500 data brokers (companies that buy and sell personal information) to delete yours. Your number gets passed around less.
    • Go to privacy.ca.gov/drop. It takes about 10 minutes.
    • First you confirm you live in California. Then you enter your name, date of birth and email. Add your phone numbers too.
    • Write down your 8-digit DROP ID, so you can check on your request later.
    • Helping a parent? In some cases, you can send a request for another California resident.

    Good to know: DROP won’t stop scammers who dial random numbers, but it helps keep your number from spreading.

  5. When a spam call gets through

    • Hang up.
    • Don’t press any buttons, even if it says “press 1 to be removed.”
    • Don’t trust caller ID. Scammers can fake any number.
    • Report the call at DoNotCall.gov.
    • Lost money? Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
    • Spam text? Forward it to 7726.
