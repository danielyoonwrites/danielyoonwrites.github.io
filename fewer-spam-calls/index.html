Fewer spam calls

Answer three quick questions. Then we’ll show you a few free steps, one at a time.

Question 1 of 3

What kind of phone do you have?

Not sure? Look at the back of your phone. An apple logo means iPhone. The word “Samsung” means Samsung Galaxy.

Question 2 of 3

Which phone company do you pay?

It’s the company on your phone bill.

Question 3 of 3

Do you live in California?

Join the Do Not Call list

  • It’s free. You only do it once.
  • From this phone, call 1-888-382-1222 and follow the steps.
  • Or sign up at DoNotCall.gov.
  • Sales calls can take up to 31 days to stop.

Scammers ignore this list, so keep going.

Turn on T-Mobile’s free Scam Block

  • Open your Phone app.
  • Dial #662# and press call.
  • That’s it. You can also turn on Scam Block in the free T-Life app.

Turn on Verizon’s free Call Filter

  • Open the Verizon Protect app.
  • Tap Services, then Call Filter, then Manage.
  • Tap Spam & category filters.
  • Turn on the Spam filter switch.

Phone can’t use that app? Use the My Verizon app instead.

Get AT&T’s free ActiveArmor app

  • Download the AT&T ActiveArmor app. It’s free.
  • It blocks fraud calls on its own.
  • In the app, tap Calls. Set Spam calls to Block.

Ask your phone company

  • Call your phone company. The number is on your bill.
  • Ask: “Do you have free spam call blocking? Can you turn it on for me?”

Quiet unknown callers on your iPhone

  • First, save your doctor, pharmacy and family as contacts, so they still ring.
  • Open Settings. Tap Apps, then Phone.
  • Find unknown callers and choose “Silence.” Their calls go to voicemail.

Turn on spam blocking on your Samsung

  • Open the Phone app.
  • Tap the three dots, then Settings.
  • Tap Caller ID and spam protection, and turn it on.
  • Turn on Block spam and scam calls.

Turn on spam filtering on your phone

  • Open the Phone app.
  • Tap the three dots, then Settings.
  • Tap Caller ID and spam.
  • Turn on “See caller ID & spam” and “Filter spam calls.”

Newer Pixel phones block spam through Call Screen instead, in the same Settings.

Use California’s free DROP tool

  • One request asks over 600 companies that sell personal information to delete yours.
  • Go to privacy.ca.gov/drop.
  • It checks that you live in California, then asks for things like your name, date of birth and email. Add your phone number too.
  • Write down the 8-digit DROP ID it gives you, and keep it private.

Helping a parent? You can send a request for them, with their permission.

When a spam call gets through

  • Hang up.
  • Don’t press any buttons, even “press 1 to be removed.”
  • Don’t trust caller ID. Scammers fake numbers.
  • Spam text? Forward it to 7726.

Want to report a call? Use DoNotCall.gov. Lost money? Use ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

You’re all set

You’ll still get a spam call now and then, but fewer of them.
